Academic Interests

⚗️ Chemistry

My interest in chemistry grew gradually through hands-on research. In my first year of high school, I investigated how caffeine affects the nervous system — analyzing its molecular structure (C₈H₁₀N₄O₄, a purine alkaloid) and conducting experiments on heart rate, oxygen saturation, and memory. In my second year, I explored the spatial protein structures of zoonotic viruses, using Biopython to visualize the 3D atomic arrangement of the rabies virus glycoprotein. By my third year, studying general and organic chemistry made me realize how deeply I find molecular-level thinking compelling. These experiences together shaped my passion for chemistry as a discipline.

HAFS Eureka · 2023
Effects of Caffeine on the Nervous System
Analyzed caffeine's chemical structure and its impact on heart rate, blood oxygen saturation, exercise performance, and memory through experiments.
HAFS Eureka · 2024
Protein Structure Analysis of Zoonotic Viruses
Predicted and visualized the 3D spatial structure of the rabies virus glycoprotein (PDB: 6LGX) using Biopython, MDAnalysis, and Matplotlib.
📐 Mathematics

Studying calculus was a turning point in how I think about problems. The elegance of differentiation and integration — the way they describe change and accumulation in the physical world — made mathematics feel less like computation and more like a language for understanding nature. This interest carried through to strong performance on the CSAT, and continues to inform how I approach quantitative problems in science and engineering.

💻 Programming

My interest in programming is rooted in its utility as a research tool rather than as an end in itself. What draws me to it is the way coding extends what is otherwise possible — enabling the visualization of protein structures, the analysis of large datasets, and experiments that would be impractical by hand. Programming allows science to scale, and that instrumental power is what I find genuinely compelling about it.
